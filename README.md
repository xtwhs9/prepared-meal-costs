## Which service deserves your first order

Start with CookUnity if you want the best odds of enjoying your meals, or Factor if you have specific nutrition targets. Multiple independent testing teams landed on the same two names.

CookUnity works with 60+ independent chefs (including recognizable names like José Garces and Esther Choi), rotates a menu that topped 400 items in a single week at last check, and ships fresh — not frozen — using modified atmosphere packaging that keeps meals fridge-fresh for 4 to 7 days. The caveats: some meals run close to 1,000 calories, some carry heavy sodium or saturated fat, and you'll want to use the nutrition filters rather than browsing hungry.

Factor, owned by HelloFresh, is the pick for keto, high-protein, or calorie-controlled eating. Every menu is dietitian-designed with 30+ weekly options, and a subscription includes a free 20-minute consultation with a nutritionist. The trade-off is commitment: you must subscribe, meals arrive in segmented plastic trays you microwave directly, and PCMag's testers occasionally found seasoning off-balance.

For narrower needs: Tempo hits at least 30 grams of protein per meal and suits GLP-1 users; Daily Harvest is the cheapest vegan option but its smoothies and soups need you to add liquid; Blue Apron is the one to pick if you refuse to be locked into a subscription.
